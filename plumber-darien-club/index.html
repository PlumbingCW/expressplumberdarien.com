Those are newer and larger homes than much of Darien. They have more bathrooms, longer pipe runs and bigger demands on a water heater. This page covers what that means in practice, and it does not assume anything is wrong with your house.

What size changes about plumbing

A large home puts more fixtures on each line and more feet of pipe between the water heater and the farthest tap. That is why people in bigger houses often notice a long wait for hot water. It is a design fact, not a defect.

A leak in a big house can also travel before it shows. Water that runs inside a wall or under a finished floor may take time to appear. If your bill rises without a change in use, start with the meter test on our leak detection page.

What the build years do and do not tell you

Homes here were built from 1993 to 2003, so they are newer than most of Darien. That does not make them trouble free, and it does not make them troubled. It means their original fixtures are now old enough to be replaced for ordinary reasons.

If a water heater is original to the house, check its label for the rated life and the date. Ask for the unit's rated life rather than guessing a number. Keep any builder or permit records you have, since they help a plumber and an inspector.

Basements, sump pumps and sewer lines

Many large homes have finished basements with bathrooms, which raises the stakes if water gets in. A tested sump pump and a working backup are worth checking before a storm. The City reported about 6 inches of rain on July 4, 2026, with roughly 2.4 inches in one hour.

A basement bathroom may rely on a sewage ejector pump, which is a different machine from a sump pump. If one stops, keep fixtures off and call. Licensed plumbers in Illinois follow the state's published standards, the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890).
