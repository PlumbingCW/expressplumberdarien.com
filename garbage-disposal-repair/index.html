Jammed, humming, leaking or dead: reading the symptoms

A disposal has only a few ways to fail. The sound it makes, or does not make, usually points to the cause.

A hum with no spinning means the grinding plate is stuck. Silence usually means a tripped reset button, a dead outlet or a failed motor. A drip from the bottom means a failed seal, and a drip from the top is often the sink flange or the drain connection.

When a disposal problem is an emergency

Most jams can wait for a regular visit. These cannot.

Clearing a jam the safe way

Turn the power off first, at the switch and the breaker. Many disposals have a hex socket on the underside, and a hex key turned back and forth frees the plate. Then press the red reset button on the bottom.

Never use your hand. If the plate still does not turn, the motor may be failing or an object may be wedged where tongs cannot reach. That is the time to call us.

What should never go in a disposal

Most repeat calls come from a short list of foods. Grease and oil cool in the pipe and stick. Coffee grounds pack into sludge. Eggshells, pasta and rice swell and bind. Fibrous scraps like celery and corn husks wrap around the shredder.

Run cold water during use and for a short time after. If your kitchen line clogs often, a hydro jetting visit can scour the pipe wall. Our drain cleaning page covers the line itself.

Repair or replace the disposal

A jam or a tripped reset is a quick repair. A leak from the body or a motor that keeps stalling usually means replacement, because the seals are part of the housing. If the unit is old and noisy, a new one may be the better choice.

When we replace one, we match the horsepower and the mounting to your sink. We also check the dishwasher knockout plug and the drain connection, which are often missed on a first try. Mounting and wiring follow the manufacturer guidelines packed with the unit.

Darien grew out of the Marion Hills, Brookhaven, Farmingdale and Hinsbrook subdivisions in the 1960s, so many kitchens in the city sit over drain lines of that era. Older cast iron and clay can be rough inside, which holds grease and food film.

Practical takeaway: if a disposal works but the sink drains slowly, the problem may be the line rather than the unit. A camera check can confirm it. See sewer camera inspection when the same drain keeps slowing down.
