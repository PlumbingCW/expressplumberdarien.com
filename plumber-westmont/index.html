Westmont's flood years and your basement

The Village of Westmont's own 2015 stormwater presentation lists a devastating flood in 2008 and historic flooding across northern Illinois in April 2013. Voters then passed a sales tax referendum on April 7, 2015, and collection began July 1, 2015, to pay for stormwater projects.

Village projects reduce street flooding but cannot stop water finding its way into a home. A sump pump on a battery backup, a clear discharge line and a working floor drain trap protect a basement. See sump pump repair and basement waterproofing.

Downtown Westmont and the sewers under it

The Downers Grove Sanitary District reports it completed sanitary sewer replacement in downtown Westmont in July 2016, including mainlines and building service pipes in the alleys. The Village then put in stormwater drainage and pervious paving there. The Village also says it built the first pervious concrete road in Illinois.

For a downtown shop or older home near those alleys, that means the public side may be newer than the private pipe inside. A backup is therefore often a problem within the building, which a camera shows. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what you will see. The District's sewer line is (630) 969-0664.

Shutoff valves worth finding before a leak

Every Westmont home has several valves that stop water fast. The main sits near the meter. Each toilet has a stop valve on the wall or floor behind the bowl, and each sink usually has two small valves under the cabinet. Water heaters have a cold supply valve on top of the tank.

Test each one once a year by turning it gently closed and open. A valve that will not move, or that drips at the stem afterward, is a repair to book on a calm day. Do not force a stuck valve. A snapped stem during an emergency turns a small leak into a flood.

A pre-storm basement checklist

Given the Village's flood history, a short checklist before heavy rain is worth the time. Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and watch the pump start, run and shut off. Confirm the discharge pipe is open at the outlet and points away from the house.

Check that the floor drain trap still holds water, since a dry trap lets sewer gas up. Keep valuables off the slab, and know where your main shutoff is. If the pump hums but does not move water, call us before the storm arrives, not during it.
