Farmingdale is another of the four subdivisions whose residents incorporated Darien in 1969. Over the years its name has stretched to cover several neighboring sections, so two Farmingdale addresses can differ a lot in age, housing type and who owns the plumbing.

The Darien Park District names parks after three of them: Pinewood Park in Farmingdale Terrace, Ridgewood Park in Farmingdale Ridge and Birchwood Park in Farmingdale. We use those, plus a local real-estate guide, to describe where each part sits. Treat the locations as approximate.

If you live in a condo or townhome

In a condominium the association usually owns the shared piping and the building's drain stacks. You usually own the plumbing inside your unit and the fixtures. The exact line is in your declaration and rules, so read those before you call anyone.

Start with the property manager when water comes through a ceiling or wall, because the source may be your neighbor's unit or a shared line. For a leak that starts and ends inside your unit, a plumber can usually go straight to work. Our leak detection page explains how a hidden leak is traced.

The July 4, 2026 storm and what it showed

The City of Darien reported that about 6 inches of rain fell on July 4, 2026, with roughly 2.4 inches in one hour. Its notice lists sump pump failure among the causes of water entering homes, and it says illegal connections of sump pump discharge into the sanitary sewer can contribute to backups.

For Farmingdale homeowners with basements, that points to a plain checklist: test the pump, confirm the discharge goes outside and keep it off the sanitary sewer. The City has asked residents to keep sump discharge at least five feet from a neighbor's property, and (630) 353-8105 is its number for questions.

How to test a sump pump before the next storm

This takes five minutes and needs no tools.

  1. 1

    Find the pit

    It is usually a round pit in the basement floor with a lid and a discharge pipe leaving the house.

  2. 2

    Check the power

    Make sure the cord is plugged into a working outlet and nothing else is on the same cord.

  3. 3

    Add water

    Pour a few buckets of water slowly into the pit and watch for the float to lift.

  4. 4

    Watch the discharge

    Outside, check that water leaves the pipe away from the foundation.

  5. 5

    Call if it fails

    A pump that does not start, or runs but moves no water, needs sump pump repair before the next storm.

Who handles the sewer, the street or the homeowner

The City's sewer page lists three sanitary sewer providers in Darien: DuPage County at (630) 964-7503, the Downers Grove Sanitary District at (630) 969-0664 and Hinsdale at (630) 323-3299. Which one serves your street depends on where you live, so we do not guess. The City will tell you at (630) 353-8105.

The sewer from the curb to the house is the homeowner's responsibility. If one drain backs up, a drain cleaning visit is the usual first step. If several fixtures back up at once, ask for a camera inspection of the lateral.

Permits and code in Farmingdale

State law requires a license for anyone who installs, alters, repairs or maintains plumbing. In Darien, plumbing changes need a permit from Community Development at 1702 Plainfield Road. Sump pump work needs a building permit with a plat of survey, per the City's permit page.

The Illinois Plumbing Code, Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code, sets the technical standards. In a condo, your association may add its own rules on top, so check before you schedule.
