Toilet safety steps before you touch anything

A few habits keep a bad toilet from turning into a bigger problem.

  • ✓
    Know the shutoff valveThe small valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet stops the fill. Turn it clockwise before you lift the tank lid or call.
  • ✓
    Skip chemical cleanersDrain openers sit in the bowl and can splash skin and eyes, and they are hard on the plumber who works next. Use a plunger instead.
  • ✓
    Watch the wet floor and outletsStanding water makes tile slick and can reach outlets and cords. Keep power strips and heaters away until the floor is dry.
  • ✓
    Treat overflow water as sewageWear rubber gloves, keep children and pets out, and wash hands and tools with soap and hot water afterward.

Running toilets and phantom flushes

A toilet that hisses or refills on its own is leaking water from the tank into the bowl. It can waste a lot of water over a month and raise your bill without any sign of a leak elsewhere.

The two most common causes are a worn flapper and a fill valve that no longer shuts off. Dropping food coloring in the tank is an easy test: if color shows in the bowl without a flush, the flapper is leaking. Replacing these parts is a short repair and usually brings the toilet back to normal.

Leaks at the base, wobble and the wax ring

A wax ring, or a similar seal, sits between the toilet and the floor flange. When it fails, water seeps out at the base, and the floor around the toilet can stay damp or feel soft.

We pull the toilet, inspect the flange and subfloor, replace the ring and set the toilet back level. If the floor is damaged or the flange is cracked, those need repair before the toilet goes back. A hidden leak may be larger than it looks, so our leak detection page explains how we trace one.

Clogged toilets and what the clog tells us

A single clogged toilet is usually a local blockage in the trap, often from too much paper, wipes or a toy. A plunger or an auger clears most of them. We use a closet auger to avoid scratching the porcelain.

If the water backs up in other fixtures, the clog sits deeper. Our drain cleaning team can open the line, and when the main is the cause, sewer line cleaning covers the buried run. Flushable wipes are a repeat cause. They often do not break down the way paper does.

Repair or replace the toilet

Not every toilet is worth fixing. Parts cost far less than a new fixture, but an old toilet with a cracked bowl or frequent clogs may be better replaced. Newer models also tend to use less water per flush than older ones. A new toilet is installed following manufacturer guidelines and the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code).

Consider replacement when the porcelain is cracked, the tank leaks from the body, parts are no longer made or the toilet clogs every week. We can set a new one the same visit. Our plumbing installation page covers new fixtures and remodel work.
