A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, using a burner or electric elements and a heat exchanger instead of a stored tank. To ask about one in Darien, call (630) 822-9290. Our line is open 24/7, so you can call about a failure at night or plan a project during the day.

Neither type is right for every home. This is how the main points line up.

What has to be right before a tankless install

Three things decide whether a tankless unit will work well. First, the gas supply must be able to feed the burner at full fire, which often means a bigger gas line than a tank needs. Second, the vent must meet the manufacturer's route and material rules. Third, the unit must be sized to the flow you want.

The unit and its vent should be installed to the manufacturer's installation guidelines, which set the gas pressure, vent material and clearances. Electric tankless units draw a lot of current, so the panel and circuit matter instead. A plumber checks all of this at a site visit, and we do it before we quote a unit rather than after.

Scale, descaling and your water

Heat exchangers are narrow, so mineral scale hurts them quickly. A descaling flush circulates a mild acid through the unit to dissolve buildup, and most makers call for it on a regular schedule.

Darien buys treated Lake Michigan water from the City of Chicago, but the published reports do not give a hardness figure we can quote. Test your tap or ask the City water operator at (630) 353-8105. If the water leaves heavy scale, a softener or filter ahead of the unit may be worth discussing.

Common tankless problems

The most common is an ignition or flame failure, often linked to a blocked vent, a closed gas valve or a dirty sensor. Next is scale, which causes overheating errors and uneven temperature.

A tankless complaint that surprises owners is the cold water sandwich. A burst of cold appears between two hot draws. A recirculation setup or a small buffer tank can reduce that, and the right choice depends on the model.

Recirculation and fast hot water

Tankless units do not store water, so long pipe runs still deliver cold water first. A recirculation pump keeps a loop of warm water moving so the tap gets hot faster. Some models have a pump built in, and others need an add-on.

Pumps can raise energy use and wear fittings if they run all the time, so timers and demand controls are common. Ask the plumber which approach fits your layout.
