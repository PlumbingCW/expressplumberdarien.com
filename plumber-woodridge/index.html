The June 2021 storm and what it taught about plumbing after damage

An EF-3 tornado struck the Woodridge area on June 20, 2021, just after 11 p.m., and damaged about 225 homes in Woodridge and neighboring suburbs. Storm damage like that can bend supply lines, crack drain stubs and knock water heater vents loose even when the house looks intact.

After any severe storm, shut the main water valve if a wall or roof is open, and keep the gas off if you smell it. Then have a plumber check the water heater, the supply pipes and the drain connections before you restore service. See our burst pipe repair page for what that looks like.

Sump pumps, downspouts and the sanitary sewer

The Village of Woodridge tells residents not to drain sump pump, pool, gutter or downspout water into the sanitary sewer. That water belongs in the storm system or the yard. A sump pump discharging into a basement drain loads the sewer in heavy rain and can push sewage back into homes on the line.

If your pump discharge is plumbed to the wrong place, or the pump is old, our sump pump repair team can reroute and test it. The Village also offers a private sanitary sewer incentive program covering overhead sewers, check valves, gate valves and sump pump installation. It requires a building permit and a licensed plumber, so call the Village first.
