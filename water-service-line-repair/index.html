Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Darien, IL

The water service line is the pipe that feeds your whole house. When it leaks, freezes or fails, you lose water or watch your meter spin. We find the fault and fix it.

The water service line runs from the city water main in the street, through a shutoff and meter, and into your home. If it is leaking or has broken in Darien, call (630) 822-9290. Our emergency line is answered 24/7, and a gushing line should not wait until morning.

While you wait, find your main shutoff, usually near the water meter in the basement, and turn it off. That stops water from entering the house. If water is flooding the yard or basement, stay away from electrical panels and outlets that are wet.

What to do right now if the line has failed

Shut off the main valve first. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, or you cannot find it, call dispatch and we will talk you through it.

Then open one faucet low in the house to relieve pressure, and take photos of any damage for your insurer. Do not dig around the line yourself, since gas, electric and other utilities may share the area.

Signs of a service line problem

Service line leaks are underground, so you often notice the effect before you find the source.

  • ✓
    Pressure drops at every faucetA leak before the house takes water out before it arrives.
  • ✓
    Meter keeps moving with everything offClose all taps and check the meter. Movement means water is going somewhere.
  • ✓
    Wet or sunken patch in the yardA soggy spot that never dries, or grass that stays greener along a line, can mark a leak.
  • ✓
    Water bill jumps without a reasonA steady leak adds up over a billing cycle.
  • ✓
    Dirty or discolored waterA break can let sediment into the line.

Repair or replace the service line

The condition of the line and the type of pipe decide. Here are the usual cases.

SituationUsual approachReason
One leak at a fitting or short sectionSpot repairThe rest of the pipe is sound
Several leaks over the yearsReplace the runRepeating repairs on aging pipe is wasteful
Old galvanized steel lineReplaceCorrosion narrows the bore and reduces flow
Suspected lead pipeReplace after identifyingLead needs proper handling and testing
Line frozen and crackedRepair or replace the damaged runCheck depth and cover as part of the fix

What Darien's own water records say

Darien buys treated Lake Michigan water from the City of Chicago, with master meters at the Plainfield Rd and 75th St pump stations. The City reports it has built a service line material inventory and is still filling in missing records.

How we fix a service line

  1. 1

    Confirm the fault

    We check the meter, the pressure and the line path to locate the leak. For tricky ones, our leak detection work narrows the spot.

  2. 2

    Locate utilities

    Underground utilities are located before any digging.

  3. 3

    Choose the method

    We compare a spot repair, a trench replacement and trenchless options.

  4. 4

    Plumbing changes in Darien need a permit, so we work with the Community Development Department. Water gets shut off for the work.

  5. 5

    We pressure-test the new pipe, flush the line and check the meter reads steady. New piping and fittings follow the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code) and the pipe manufacturer's guidelines for joints.

Lead pipes and your water line

If your line is old, you may wonder whether it contains lead. Only testing and a look at the pipe can say for sure. Do not assume either way.

If we find lead during a repair, we will explain it and talk through replacement. For a broader replacement of failing supply lines inside the house, our whole house repiping page explains that work.

Freezing, low pressure and what to check first

Not every pressure problem is the service line. Check the main valve first, since a partly closed valve restricts flow. If only the hot side is weak, the issue may be the water heater, and our water heater repair page can help.

If pressure is low everywhere and the meter runs, the service line becomes the likely suspect.
