When a leak is an emergency

A slow drip under a sink can wait for a normal appointment. These signs mean water is moving now and the damage grows by the hour.

  • ✓
    Water is staining a ceiling or wallA brown spot that spreads, bubbles paint or feels soft means a supply or drain line is wet behind the surface. Shut off the main valve first.
  • ✓
    You hear running water with everything offA hiss or trickle at night, when no fixture is in use, usually points to a pressurized line leaking inside a wall or under the floor.
  • ✓
    A floor feels warm or looks dampA warm spot on a floor can mean a hot water line is leaking below it. Damp carpet or lifted flooring near a wall points the same way.
  • ✓
    The meter keeps spinningIf the meter moves while every fixture is off, water is leaving the house somewhere. The test below confirms it in a few minutes.

A single faucet drip with no stain and no sound is a regular visit. Tell dispatch which case you have and we will schedule it to match.

The two-minute water meter test

Your water meter can tell you whether a leak exists before anyone visits. Most Darien homes have the meter in the basement near where the line enters the house. Read it carefully, because this test saves a lot of guessing.

  1. 1

    Turn everything off

    Stop the washer, dishwasher, ice maker and irrigation. Make sure nobody flushes or runs a tap.

  2. 2

    Find the leak indicator

    Many meters have a small triangle or dial that spins when any water moves. Note its position or the last digits of the reading.

  3. 3

    Wait and look again

    Check after fifteen to thirty minutes with nothing on. Any movement means water is leaving the house.

  4. 4

    Close the main valve and recheck

    If the dial stops, the leak is inside the home. If it keeps moving with the valve closed, the valve or the meter needs attention, so call us.

Slab leaks: what they are and how we confirm one

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe that runs in or beneath a concrete floor. It is most common in homes built on a slab, and in the slab sections of homes with basements, such as an addition or an attached garage floor.

Signs that point to a slab leak

The usual clues are a warm patch on the floor, the sound of water with all fixtures off, a meter that keeps moving, and damp flooring that has no clear source overhead. A sudden drop in water pressure can add to the picture.

How we confirm it

We isolate sections of the supply piping, pressure test each one, and listen along the floor with sensitive equipment. A thermal camera can show a warm line. Once the area is narrow, the next step is a repair plan that may be a spot repair, a reroute or a repipe if the piping is failing in several places.

How we find a leak without opening every wall

Good leak detection moves from the least invasive information to the most invasive. We start with the meter and your description of the symptoms. Then we shut off sections of the house and watch whether pressure holds.

For leaks hidden in walls we use acoustic listening and thermal imaging. In some cases a plumber may use a tracer gas method. A camera inspection of drain lines through a sewer camera inspection can reveal leaks on the waste side, which are easy to confuse with supply leaks.

We open the surface only after the location is confirmed, and we keep the opening as small as the repair allows. Any repair that follows is done to the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code).

Lake Michigan water and what your meter means

Darien buys treated Lake Michigan water from the City of Chicago, with master meters at the Plainfield Road and 75th Street pump stations. Your home meter is the last measuring point before the water enters your piping, so it is the cleanest place to test for loss.

If your meter test shows movement and you need to ask about your account or meter, call the City water operator at (630) 353-8105. Then call us to trace the leak itself. The City reads the meter. It does not repair pipes inside your home.

Outdoor and underground leaks

A line that runs from the meter to the house is the homeowner's responsibility in most cases, so confirm responsibility with the City before assuming. Signs of a buried leak include a wet strip in the lawn, a sunken spot in a driveway or a drop in pressure at every tap.

When the leak sits on that run, our water service line repair team can plan the fix after the leak is found. Pointing out the meter location and any clean-out nearby helps the plumber start faster.
