Commercial plumbing covers the water, drain and gas piping in shops, offices, restaurants and multi-unit buildings, plus the equipment that protects them. If you have an urgent failure right now, call (630) 822-9290. Our emergency line is open 24/7 and a plumber can be sent after hours.

Commercial work is not scaled-up residential work

A commercial building has bigger pipe, more fixtures per branch and often a shared system between tenants. A clog in one unit can show up in another, and a shutoff may serve a whole floor.

That is why we ask on the first call how many fixtures are affected, whether you own or lease, and who controls the building shutoffs. Those answers decide who we need on site.

Which system is which

Commercial properties often have several water-related systems. Each has its own risks and its own service.

SystemWhat it doesWhat goes wrong
Domestic waterBrings potable water to taps and equipmentLeaks, low pressure, failed valves
Sanitary drainsCarry waste to the sewerClogs, roots, offsets, grease
Backflow preventersStop dirty water flowing back into the supplyFailed tests, leaking relief ports
Grease traps and interceptorsCatch fats before they reach the sewerOverflow, odor, full tank
Storm and roof drainsCarry rain off roofs and lotsDebris, ice, backing up
Gas pipingFeeds heaters, ranges and rooftop unitsLeaks, failed connectors

Which of these your building has depends on its use and age.

Backflow prevention

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that keeps water from reversing and pulling contaminated water back into the building supply. You see them on irrigation lines, fire lines, boilers, car washes and food service equipment.

Many water suppliers require periodic testing by a qualified tester, and a failed device may need repair or replacement. Rules differ by system and by use, so ask the City water operator at (630) 353-8105 what applies to your property. If you have a device that drips from a relief port, call us for a check. Device choice and installation follow the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code) and the device's manufacturer guidelines.

Grease traps and interceptors

Restaurants and kitchens send fats and food solids into the drain. A grease trap or interceptor sits in the line to catch them before they reach the sewer.

When one is full, the line downstream clogs, the kitchen smells and sinks drain slowly. The device needs regular pumping, and the schedule depends on how much you cook. After a blockage, a hydro jetting pass scours the line clean, and a camera shows whether the trap is working.

Storm drains and roof drains

Storm drains handle rain, not waste. Commercial roofs often have internal drains or downspouts that tie to a storm line, and parking lots have catch basins.

Leaves, silt and trash block them, and a flat roof with a blocked drain can pond water fast. Clear the debris from roof screens when it is safe. If water backs up in a basement or a catch basin overflows, we cable or jet the line and look at it on camera. Drainage from a sump belongs on its own line, see sump pump repair.
