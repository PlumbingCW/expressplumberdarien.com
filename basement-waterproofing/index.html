Basement waterproofing, on the plumbing side, means controlling water that enters and moving it out through drains and a sump pump. If water is on your floor now, call (630) 822-9290. Our 24/7 line is open, and dispatch will say what to do while you wait.

Planned waterproofing is booked by appointment. Before we visit, note where and when the water shows up, such as after rain, after snowmelt or all the time. That pattern tells us more than a photo of a dry floor.

First question: where is the water coming from

Not all basement water is the same, and the fix depends on the source. Groundwater, a surface problem outside, a plumbing leak and a sewer backup all look alike on the floor.

We start by working out which one you have. It prevents spending on a drain system when the real issue is a downspout dumping at the wall.

Interior drains and the sump pit

An interior drain system collects water at the base of the wall and sends it to a sump pit, where a pump lifts it out of the house. Done well, it relieves the pressure that pushes water through the floor joint.

The pump is the heart of it. If the pump is weak or the discharge is poor, the best drain channel will still fail. Our sump pump repair page covers pumps, battery backup and discharge lines in more detail.

Floor drains and backwater

Floor drains handle laundry and spills, but they are also an open path for sewer water in a backup. A drain that starts to bubble in a storm tells you the main line is stressed.

Cleaning the line helps, and a camera shows whether roots or a low spot are the cause. A sewer line cleaning visit is the first step when the drain is slow or backs up. Ask about a backwater valve if backups have repeated, since it is a device that blocks reverse flow. Placement and installation follow the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code) and the valve maker's instructions.

What an older Darien basement tells us

Darien's subdivisions, including Marion Hills, Brookhaven, Farmingdale and Hinsbrook, date from the 1960s onward. Basements from that era may have drain tile around the footing that has silted or been cut off over time.

That is worth knowing because a modern interior system often works with the old tile instead of replacing it. We check how water moves before choosing. If your downspouts tie into an old drain, that tie-in is the first place to look. Sump water should not go to the sanitary sewer, per City of Darien guidance.
