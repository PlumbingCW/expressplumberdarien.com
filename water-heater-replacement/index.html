While you wait, shut the cold water valve above the heater and turn off the power or set the gas control to off. Open a hot tap upstairs so the tank does not build pressure. Move boxes and stored items away from the heater so the plumber has clear access.

Signs the tank is finished

A tank that leaks from the shell is done. Rust at the seams, a wet floor that returns after you wipe it, or water weeping from the bottom jacket all mean the steel has failed.

Other signs are softer. Rusty hot water, a rumbling tank that no longer reaches temperature, and repeated repairs in a short stretch all point toward replacement. Our water heater repair page covers the cases where fixing a part still makes sense.

Safety steps when a water heater fails

Most failures leave you without hot water but are not dangerous. These need quick action.

  • ✓
    Water is spreading across the floorShut the cold supply, cut power or gas, and call. Finished basements and carpet absorb water fast.
  • ✓
    The tank is hot and the relief valve is dischargingKeep everyone back from the discharge pipe and call right away.
  • ✓
    Gas smell near the heaterLeave and call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Do not try to relight anything.
  • ✓
    Scorch marks or soot on the cabinet or flueSwitch the heater off and call. This points to combustion or venting trouble.

Sizing: how big a tank do you need

Sizing follows the number of people, the number of bathrooms, and how many hot draws overlap in the morning. A larger tank holds more reserve. A faster recovery rate refills it sooner.

Do not just copy the old label. If the household has grown, or shrunk, the old size may be wrong. Tell the plumber about showers, soaking tubs and the dishwasher schedule, and let them match the tank to the real load.

What a proper change-out includes

A quality swap is more than a new tank. The plumber should replace worn supply connectors, check the relief valve and its discharge pipe, confirm the venting on gas units, and look at the drain pan and shutoff valves.

Many homes also need an expansion tank to absorb thermal expansion when water heats in a closed system. Ask whether yours has one and whether it is still working. Our water heater installation page covers the connections and code side in more detail.

The new unit should be set and connected to the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code) and the manufacturer's installation guidelines. Ask to see the plumber's license before work starts. The state offers a public verification tool.
