Marion Hills is one of the four subdivisions whose residents incorporated Darien in 1969. A neighborhood directory says it dates back to 1949 and lists homes built anywhere from 1940 to 2007. That wide range is the most useful fact for a homeowner here.

Why homes on one street can have different plumbing

A neighborhood that grew from 1949 and kept building for decades is a patchwork. A 1950s house, a later rebuild and a newer infill home can share a block. Each may have different supply pipe, drain material and water heater age.

That is the reason not to compare notes with the neighbor. What worked for their house may not apply to yours. A plumber checks your own lines, and the useful starting point is your own repair history and your basement.

Roots, mature trees and the line to the street

The City of Darien's sewer page says tree roots can damage or clog sewers in areas with mature trees. Established neighborhoods tend to have them, and a neighborhood directory says Marion Hills dates back to 1949. If a drain has clogged more than once, roots are one of the first things to check.

A sewer camera inspection shows what is in the line and where. If the camera finds a root mass, hydro jetting can clear it. If it finds a break, the fix is a repair, and you will want to see the video before deciding.

Replacing parts, not whole systems

In a house that has been added to over decades, the usual job is a repair or a swap, not a rebuild. A failed toilet fill valve, a dripping shutoff or a tired water heater are separate jobs. Our water heater replacement page and toilet repair page cover the common ones.

If you see rusty water, low pressure or repeat pinhole leaks across the house, say so on the call. Those can point to a bigger supply problem, and a plumber can tell you whether a section or the whole line is the issue. That is a decision to make with real numbers, not a guess.

Permits and who to ask

Anyone who installs, alters, repairs or maintains plumbing in Illinois needs a state license. Darien's Community Development Department at 1702 Plainfield Road issues permits when plumbing is changed, and it is the place to ask if a job needs one.

Technical standards for the work come from Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code, known as the Illinois Plumbing Code. Routine clearing of a clog does not usually change the plumbing, but repairs and replacements often do.
