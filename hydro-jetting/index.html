Hydro jetting uses a hose and a nozzle that fires high-pressure water in all directions inside a drain, scouring grease, scale and roots from the pipe wall. If a line in Darien is blocked and the problem keeps returning, call (630) 822-9290. The emergency line is answered 24/7.

If a drain is backing up now, stop adding water and do not pour chemical openers into it. A jetter works best when the line has not been filled with caustic liquid, and the plumber's safety depends on it. Tell dispatch if you already used a product.

Jetting compared with snaking

Both tools clear clogs, but they work differently. The choice depends on what is in the pipe.

Cable (snake)Hydro jetting
How it worksPokes or cuts through the blockageScours the full pipe wall with water
Best forA single soft plugGrease, scale, heavy roots and repeat clogs
What stays behindA pipe wall that is still coatedA cleaner, wider bore
Pipe condition neededTolerates older pipe wellPipe should be sound, so camera first
Typical outcomeQuick reliefLonger interval between clogs

Neither tool repairs a broken pipe. Both are cleaning methods.

When jetting is the better choice

We recommend jetting when a line clogs repeatedly, when grease from a kitchen is the cause, or when roots have thickened the opening. Mineral scale in older iron pipes narrows the bore over time, and jetting can open it again.

A single clogged bathroom sink rarely needs it. Our drain cleaning page covers the lighter work that handles most household clogs.

When jetting is not the right tool

Water at high pressure is powerful, and some pipes should not see it. We check first.

  • ✓
    A collapsed or cracked pipePressure can push water out of the break and wash soil away. A repair comes first.
  • ✓
    Badly offset jointsThe nozzle can snag, and cutting heads can catch on the lip.
  • ✓
    Thin or brittle old pipeA pipe at the end of its life can fail under force. The camera tells us if it is safe.
  • ✓
    Inside walls without a cleanoutWithout proper access, jetting is risky. We find or create a safe access point.

What the jetting visit looks like

  1. 1

    Camera first

    We run a camera to see the pipe material, the blockage and any damage.

  2. 2

    Set up the jetter

    The hose enters through a cleanout, with the nozzle chosen for grease, scale or roots.

  3. 3

    Work back from the clog

    We push in, then pull back while spinning water scours the walls.

  4. 4

    Water runs at several fixtures to confirm full flow.

    A second look shows the pipe wall so you can see the difference.

Why jetting suits some Darien properties

Darien's older neighborhoods, such as Marion Hills, Brookhaven, Farmingdale and Hinsbrook, were built up around the 1960s. Pipes from that period may carry decades of mineral scale and grease film, and roots can find the joints.

For a home in one of those areas that has needed cleaning more than once, jetting plus a camera is often a better use of money than another quick cable pass. The video decides whether jetting or repair makes sense. We set nozzle type and pressure within the jetter manufacturer's guidelines and the condition of the pipe.

Jetting and maintenance schedules

Some lines benefit from a recurring service. A kitchen line that takes heavy cooking grease, or a main with a root history, may be jetted every year or two. The camera shows how fast buildup returns, so the schedule fits the pipe instead of a calendar.

If jetting reveals roots keep returning, a sewer line repair may close the entry point for good.
