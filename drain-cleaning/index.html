When a clogged drain is an emergency

Most slow drains can wait a day. A few cannot, and the signs are easy to spot.

  • ✓
    More than one fixture is affectedA toilet that gurgles when the shower drains, or a basement floor drain that burbles when the washer runs, points to the main line rather than a single trap.
  • ✓
    Sewage is coming upDark, smelly water in a tub, shower or floor drain is a health issue. Keep children and pets out and call right away.
  • ✓
    Water is already on the floorA kitchen sink that overflows onto cabinets or flooring can ruin materials in a few hours. Shut the fixture's supply and call.
  • ✓
    The clog came back within daysA line that clears for a short time and then fails again usually has roots, grease buildup or a belly in the pipe, not just a plug.

Kitchen sink and disposal clogs

Kitchen lines clog differently from bathroom lines. Cooking grease cools inside the pipe and traps coffee grounds, rice and eggshells until the opening is the size of a straw.

We cable the kitchen branch from the trap or the nearest cleanout, then run hot water to carry loosened grease out. If the sink drains but the disposal side stays blocked, the problem is often the disposal outlet or the dishwasher hose connection. Our garbage disposal repair page covers jams and leaks at the unit itself.

Snaking, cabling and hydro jetting: choosing the right tool

A cable machine, sometimes called a snake, pokes a hole through a clog. It is quick and works well on hair, paper and soft plugs in fixture lines. It does not scrape the pipe wall clean, so grease and scale can return.

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full pipe wall. We recommend it when a line clogs repeatedly, when grease is the cause, or when roots have thickened the opening. It is not for every pipe, and a camera look first tells us whether the line can handle the pressure.

Plain cabling stays the first choice for a single fixture. A camera plus jetting is the better answer once the same drain has failed twice in a season.

What 1960s Darien subdivisions mean for your drains

Darien was incorporated in 1969 by residents of the Marion Hills, Brookhaven, Farmingdale and Hinsbrook subdivisions, so many homes in the city date from the 1960s onward. Homes from that era were often built with cast iron or clay tile under the slab or basement floor, and those materials can roughen, crack or let roots in over decades.

The practical takeaway is simple. If a main line has clogged more than once, ask for a camera look rather than another quick cable. The video shows whether the line has a root mass, an offset joint or a low spot that holds water, and that decides between cleaning and repair.

A drain that backs up only during storms is a different case. Heavy runoff can stress sewer mains, and sump pump water sent into household drains makes it worse. The City of Darien has told residents to keep sump discharge off the sanitary sewer and to call (630) 353-8105 with questions.

Floor drains, yard drains and window wells

Basement floor drains collect lint, sediment and sometimes grease from a laundry tub. We clean them with a small cable or a jetter nozzle and check that the trap still holds water, since a dry trap lets sewer smell into the house.

Outdoor yard drains and window well drains carry leaves, soil and roots instead. They are a separate system from your household sewer, and clearing them keeps water from standing against the foundation. Tell dispatch which one you have, because the tools and the approach differ.

A drain line that leaks

Sometimes the clog is not the only problem. A damp ceiling under a bathroom, a wet spot along a basement wall or a musty smell near a drain can mean the drain pipe itself is leaking. We will point out signs of a drain line leak while the camera is in the line, and our leak detection service can trace a leak that is not obvious.
