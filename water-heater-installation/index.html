Water heater installation is the planned placement and hookup of a new unit, including the supply lines, fuel or power, venting, relief valve piping and a drain pan where needed. Call (630) 822-9290 to schedule one. Our line is open 24/7, so you can also reach us outside normal business hours.

Before the visit, clear a three-foot path to the old heater or the planned spot, and take a photo of the rating plate if you can. That plate tells the plumber the size, fuel and model, which saves time at the door.

Who needs a planned installation

Most installs follow a failure, but plenty are planned. A remodel may move the heater. A new addition may need a second unit. A homeowner may want to switch fuel before the old tank gives out.

Planning gives you time to compare types, choose the location and have the right permit handled before the old tank leaks. If the old heater has already failed, our water heater replacement page is the faster path.

Safety steps on every install

These checks protect your household, and you can ask the plumber to walk you through each one.

  • ✓
    Gas smell means leaveIf you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Do not touch switches or try to relight anything.
  • ✓
    Vent and combustion airGas units need a sound vent path and enough air to burn cleanly. A blocked or leaking vent can put carbon monoxide in the house.
  • ✓
    Relief valve dischargeThe temperature and pressure relief valve needs a discharge pipe routed down to a safe spot, with no cap or plug on it.
  • ✓
    Power and fuel off firstBefore any connection is opened, the breaker is off or the gas is shut at the unit. Power returns only after the tank is full.

Installation, in the order it happens

The order matters because each connection depends on the one before it.

  1. 1

    Walk-through

    The plumber checks the location, floor, venting path, drain and fuel or electric supply.

  2. 2

    Shutoff and drain

    Water is shut off, the old unit is drained and disconnected.

  3. 3

    Set and level

    The new unit goes on a proper pan or platform and is leveled.

  4. 4

    Connect

    Water lines, gas or electric, flue or vent, and relief valve discharge are connected.

  5. 5

    Fill and purge

    The tank is filled, air is purged from the lines and all joints are checked for leaks.

  6. 6

    Start and test

    Burner or elements start, temperature is set, and safety devices are tested.

Venting and combustion air on gas units

Gas heaters make exhaust that has to leave the house. An atmospheric unit uses a draft hood and a vertical flue that relies on rising warm air. A power-vent unit pushes exhaust out through plastic pipe with a fan.

Each design has its own routing rules, and a flue that worked for the old tank may not suit the new one. This is why the installer checks the vent pipe, the clearances and the air supply to the room. Never skip it, because carbon monoxide has no smell.

Illinois and Darien rules

Illinois requires a license for anyone who installs plumbing, and the work is measured against the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code) plus the manufacturer's installation guidelines for your unit. The state offers a public license verification tool, and you should use it for any plumber you hire.

In Darien, permits are required whenever plumbing is changed, and contractors register their state license with the City. Water heater permits are not listed on the City's page, so ask the Community Development Department at 1702 Plainfield Rd before the install is scheduled.
