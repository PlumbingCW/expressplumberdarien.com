Frozen Pipe Repair: What to Do Right Now

A frozen pipe is a problem. A frozen pipe that cracks is a flood. Here is the order of operations that protects the house.

Do these things first

If a faucet gives only a trickle or nothing, a pipe may be frozen. Act now, before it thaws on its own and bursts.

  • ✓
    Open the faucetLeave the tap on, even a trickle. This lets steam and pressure escape as the ice melts.
  • ✓
    Find the main shutoffKnow where it is. If a pipe bursts, turn it off right away.
  • ✓
    Look for the frozen spotCheck pipes along exterior walls, in the garage, in the crawlspace and near the basement ceiling.
  • ✓
    Warm the roomTurn up the heat and open cabinet doors under sinks so warm air reaches the pipes.
  • ✓
    Call if you see waterA wet ceiling, a bulge or a drip means the pipe has split. Shut off the main and call (630) 822-9290.

Dispatch is open 24/7, nights and weekends included. If water is already moving, say so right away.

How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

Only thaw a pipe that you can reach, and only if there is no sign of a split. Go slowly.

  1. 1

    Keep the tap open

    Water must have a way out as it thaws.

  2. 2

    Apply gentle heat

    Use a hair dryer on low or warm towels. Start at the faucet end and work toward the frozen part.

  3. 3

    Be patient

    It can take a while. Do not rush it with more heat.

  4. 4

    Check for leaks

    Once water flows, look along the pipe and the ceiling and walls nearby for any drip.

  5. 5

    Leave it flowing a little

    A slow trickle of water on the next cold night can help keep it from refreezing.

What not to do

Some thaw methods turn a nuisance into a fire or an injury.

  • ✓
    No open flameA torch, a lighter or a camp stove on a pipe can scorch framing and start a fire.
  • ✓
    No space heater under a wet areaKeep heaters away from water and from anything that can burn.
  • ✓
    No boiling water on a pipeA sudden temperature jump can crack a pipe or a fixture.
  • ✓
    No walking awayA thawing pipe can burst and flood a room while you are in another.
  • ✓
    No chemicalsChemical products do nothing for ice in a supply line.

If you cannot reach the frozen section, or it is behind a wall or under a slab, call. Our burst pipe repair team can thaw and repair lines that you cannot get to.

Where pipes tend to freeze and what to do

Freezing follows cold air. The places that feel drafty are the usual suspects.

LocationWhy it freezesWhat helps
Exterior wallsCold air reaches the pipe through the wall cavityOpen the cabinet door and heat the room
GarageOften unheated and draftyClose the door, run a small heater away from water
Crawlspace or basement ceilingCold air from vents or gapsSeal gaps and add insulation
Outdoor hose bibsThe pipe sits in the cold wallDisconnect hoses in fall
Attic runsExposed to the coldest airAdd insulation and keep the attic closed

Which pipes in your home are at risk depends on how the house was built and where the pipes run.

After the thaw: check for damage

A pipe that froze may have cracked even if it is not leaking yet. Ice can split a pipe along its length, and the leak only shows once the ice melts and pressure returns. Watch the area for an hour or two after thawing.

Look for damp drywall, a stain that spreads or a hissing sound. If water appears and you cannot see where it comes from, our leak detection team can trace it.

A replacement section should be installed to the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code) and insulated as the pipe maker's manufacturer guidelines direct.

How to keep it from happening again

Insulate exposed pipes where they run through cold spaces. Seal gaps where cold air enters near pipes. On very cold nights, open cabinet doors and let a faucet drip.

If a pipe in an outside wall freezes more than once, ask about rerouting or insulating it. Our whole house repiping page covers larger fixes for failing supply piping.
