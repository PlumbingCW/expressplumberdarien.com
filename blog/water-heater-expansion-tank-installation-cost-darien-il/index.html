The short answer on expansion tank cost

An expansion tank install is usually one of the smaller jobs a plumber does on a water heater. The tank itself is a modest part. The price moves with how easy the connection is to reach, whether the heater is being replaced at the same time and whether the house needs other fixes first.

Plenty of homes in Darien already have one. Many do not, and a lot of homeowners find out only when a relief valve drips. If you want a firm number for your house, call (630) 822-9290 and describe the heater. You will hear a clear price before work starts.

The rest of this page explains what the tank does, which factors change the cost and what a Darien homeowner should check before calling.

What the tank does inside your plumbing

Water expands when it heats. In an open system the extra volume just pushes back toward the street. In a closed system it has nowhere to go, so pressure climbs every time the burner or element runs.

  • ✓
    It absorbs the extra volumeA rubber diaphragm splits the tank into a water side and an air side. The air cushion gives the expanding water somewhere to go.
  • ✓
    It protects the relief valveWithout a cushion, the temperature and pressure relief valve on the heater may open again and again. A drip at that valve is a common first clue.
  • ✓
    It protects the rest of the houseRepeated pressure spikes stress supply lines, faucet cartridges, toilet fill valves and the heater itself.

The tank does not replace the relief valve. It works alongside it. Never cap or plug a dripping relief valve. Call a plumber instead.

Does your house have a closed system?

A closed system happens when something stops water from flowing backward into the main. Here are the usual causes and what each one means for your tank.

What is on your supply lineEffect on pressureWhat it means for an expansion tank
A check valve or backflow preventer at the meterBlocks backflow, so the system is closedA tank is normally needed
A pressure reducing valve on the mainOften acts like a check valveA tank is normally needed
No device and an open path to the mainExpanding water can drift backA tank may not be needed, a plumber confirms
A new meter or new valve installed by the utilityMay have changed an open system to closedRecheck even if you never needed a tank before

Do not guess from the table. A plumber with a pressure gauge on a hose bib can tell you what your house is doing.

That last row matters. A house that ran fine for years can become closed after a utility or plumbing change.

What Darien's water supply means for your tank

Darien buys treated Lake Michigan water from the City of Chicago, and the two master meters sit at the Plainfield Road and 75th Street pump stations. Your own meter and the valves around it are a separate matter. That is the part that decides whether your system is closed.

So the useful move is to look near your meter and main shutoff for a check valve or a pressure reducing valve. If you see one, assume the system is closed and check the heater for an expansion tank. What changes the cost of the install

Every house is a little different. These are the things a plumber weighs when giving you a price.

Tank sizeTanks are sized to the heater's capacity and the house pressure, so a larger heater may need a larger tank.
Access and spaceA clear spot above or beside the heater is quick. A cramped utility room or a finished ceiling takes more time.
Existing fittingsIf the cold line has no tee to attach to, the plumber has to add one, which means more work on the pipe.
Standalone or combined visitAdding a tank during a water heater installation is simpler than a separate trip.
Related problemsA failed pressure reducing valve, high house pressure or a corroded fitting can add repair steps before the tank goes on.
Hour of the callA weekday booking and an after-hours dispatch are scheduled differently.
A homeowners policy usually covers sudden water damage but often excludes wear, corrosion and slow leaks. If a heater has already leaked, take photos before cleanup, keep receipts and ask your insurer what applies.

Safety steps before anyone touches the heater

Water heaters combine heat, pressure and power or gas. A few rules keep a simple job from becoming an injury.

  • ✓
    Power or gas off firstSwitch off the breaker for an electric unit. For gas, turn the control to off and close the gas valve. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the gas utility.
  • ✓
    Relieve pressure before opening anythingClose the cold supply and open a hot tap so pressure drops before any fitting is loosened.
  • ✓
    Respect the scald riskWater in the tank and the first draw from the relief pipe can be hot enough to burn. Let it cool and keep hands and faces clear.
  • ✓
    Leave the T&P valve aloneNever cap, plug or tighten shut the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe.

Signs your water heater may already need an expansion tank

You do not have to wait for a failure. A few clues point to thermal expansion trouble.

Dripping at the relief valve

A little water at the discharge pipe each time the heater cycles is the classic sign. If the drip never stops, the valve itself may be bad, which is a repair for water heater repair.

Banging pipes after a heating cycle

A thump when hot water is first used or after the burner shuts off can come from pressure swings, though loose pipe straps cause the same noise. A plumber sorts out which.

Short-lived faucets and fill valves

If cartridges and toilet parts fail more often than they should, high pressure may be the quiet cause.

How the installation goes

  1. 1

    Check the pressure

    The plumber puts a gauge on a hose bib and reads the house pressure. The tank's air charge must match it.

  2. 2

    Shut off and drain down

    Water to the heater is closed, power or gas is made safe and a short section of cold line is drained.

  3. 3

    Add the connection

    A tee goes on the cold supply near the heater, with a support so the tank's weight does not strain the pipe.

  4. 4

    Set the air charge

    The tank's air side is adjusted to the measured house pressure before it is put in service.

  5. 5

    Refill and test

    The system is filled, purged of air and checked for leaks, and the relief valve is looked over.

Common mistakes with expansion tanks

The biggest one is skipping the pressure check. A tank shipped with a factory air charge that does not match your house will not work properly. The second is mounting the tank so its full weight hangs on a copper line with no support. The third is treating a dripping relief valve as a nuisance and plugging it.

Support, fittings and relief valve discharge should follow the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code) and the tank maker's manufacturer guidelines.

Replacement matters too. An expansion tank can lose its air or its diaphragm and become waterlogged. Tap it. A tank that sounds dull and solid all the way down is a candidate for replacement.
