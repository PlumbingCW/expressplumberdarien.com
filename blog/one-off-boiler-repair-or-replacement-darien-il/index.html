One-Off Boiler Repair or Replacement: How to Decide

A single failed part is not a dead boiler. These are the questions that separate a one-time repair from a unit that is nearing the end.

Start with the pattern, not the breakdown

A boiler that fails once, on a cold night, because of a worn circulator pump or a bad thermostat is a repair case more often than not. A boiler that has failed three times in two winters, leaks from the heat exchanger or is rusting at the base is a different story.

So the useful question is not whether it broke. It is why it broke, whether the part is the cause or a symptom, and what else is close behind. A plumber who has opened the unit can answer that. If your boiler has quit and the house is cooling, call (630) 822-9290. Dispatch is open 24/7.

Repair and replace side by side

No chart decides this for you, but it shows which way each factor tends to lean.

This is a general guide. The decision depends on your unit, your fuel and your home.

Warning signs that point toward replacement

One or two of these do not decide it, but together they are a case for looking at a new unit.

  • ✓
    Leaks from the boiler bodyA leak at a fitting is usually fixable. Water seeping from the heat exchanger or the shell usually is not.
  • ✓
    Rust and mineral buildupFlaking, stains or heavy scale on the unit or the piping suggest long-term water chemistry problems.
  • ✓
    Frequent pressure and relief valve troubleA valve that keeps dripping or a system that keeps losing pressure points to a deeper issue.
  • ✓
    Repeated lockouts and short cyclingA boiler that shuts itself down or turns on and off rapidly may have a failing control or a sizing problem.
  • ✓
    Noise from the unitKettling, banging or rumbling can come from scale in the heat exchanger.

Our boiler repair team can look at the unit and tell you which column you are in.

What a plumber checks before giving you a recommendation

  1. 1

    Cause of the failure

    Was it the part that failed, or something upstream such as water quality, pressure or a cracked section?

  2. 2

    Visible condition

    Rust, leaks, scorch marks, corrosion on the flue or the combustion chamber.

  3. 3

    System pressure and water level

    Low or fluctuating readings can mean a leak in the system, not the boiler.

  4. 4

    Safety devices

    Relief valve, low-water cutoff and flue draft are checked. A problem here moves the decision.

  5. 5

    Parts and rated life

    Whether parts are available and what the manufacturer rates for the unit.

Questions that protect you from a bad call

Ask the plumber what exactly failed and whether a photo is available. Ask what else they looked at while the unit was open. Ask what the repair covers and whether any new problem would be a separate charge.

Ask for the unit's rated life from the maker, and ask when the unit was installed if you do not know. Do not accept a firm replace-or-repair verdict without seeing what the plumber saw. You can always call back, and a second opinion is reasonable for a replacement of this size.

A new boiler should be installed to the maker's manufacturer guidelines and to the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code), which governs plumbing work in the state.

Do not forget the water side

A boiler is only one part of a hydronic system. If the piping, radiators or baseboard are old, a new boiler can expose leaks that the old unit's lower pressure hid. A plumber who talks about the whole loop is thinking ahead.
