Does Sand Clog Drains? Yes, and Here Is Why

Sand does not dissolve and it does not float. In a pipe it settles, packs and traps whatever else comes along.

Yes, sand clogs drains, but slowly and in particular places

Sand is heavy and does not dissolve. A little of it rinses through a sink, but sand carried by slow water settles at low points in the pipe. Over time, the layer thickens and catches hair, grease and paper that would otherwise pass.

That is why a sand clog often builds quietly. The drain gets a bit slower, then a bit slower, and then stops. Sand also wears on fixtures and can scratch the finish of a tub or a basin.

Where sand comes from and where it ends up

Most sand does not come from a beach vacation. It enters the plumbing in ordinary ways.

SourceHow it gets inWhere it settles
Beach or sandbox playRinsing kids or gear in a tub or utility sinkTrap, then the first low spot in the branch

What to do if you think sand is in the line

  1. 1

    Stop adding more

    Do not rinse more sandy items down the drain. Move rinse jobs outside to a hose and a bucket.

  2. 2

    Check the trap

    If you can reach a sink trap, place a bucket under it and unscrew the nut. Sand often shows up there first.

  3. 3

    Skip chemical cleaners

    They do not dissolve sand and can sit in the pipe. Tell the plumber if you used one.

  4. 4

    Call for a cable or jetter

    A cable can loosen a plug. A hydro jetter flushes sand out of the pipe wall and low spots.

  5. 5

    Ask for a camera if it repeats

    Sand that keeps coming back can mean a low spot in the pipe where it collects.

What this means in Darien

Waterfall Glen Forest Preserve borders the south edge of Darien, and trail days mean muddy boots and gritty gear. Rinse them outside, not in a basement utility sink or over a floor drain. Fine grit from trails behaves like sand once it reaches a trap.

Many homes in Darien date from the 1960s and later, after the Marion Hills, Brookhaven, Farmingdale and Hinsbrook subdivisions grew up. Older basements often have floor drains and sump pits that collect grit. If your floor drain slows down after a rain, check whether sediment is coming in.

The City of Darien has told residents to keep sump pump discharge off the sanitary sewer. A sump discharge that carries sediment into the wrong line adds to the problem. Call (630) 353-8105 with questions about it.

Sand in sump pits and window wells

A sump pit is meant to catch groundwater, and some of that water carries fine sand and silt. Grit that collects on the pit floor can jam the float switch or wear out the pump. A pit that looks like a sandbox is overdue for cleaning.

A window well drain gets leaves and soil along with sand. If water stands in the well after rain, clear the surface debris and call if the drain stays slow. Our sump pump repair page covers pumps that will not start or run constantly.

Your pump maker's manufacturer guidelines say how often to inspect the pit and the intake, so follow them.

How plumbers clear a sand clog

The goal is to get the sand out of the pipe, not to push it farther down.

  • ✓
    Trap cleaningWhere possible, the trap is removed and emptied by hand.
  • ✓
    CablingA cable breaks up compacted material and pulls some of it out.
  • ✓
    Hydro jettingHigh-pressure water flushes sand out of the line. The hydro jetting page explains where it fits and where it does not.
  • ✓
    Camera checkA camera shows whether sand is still sitting in a low spot.

Pushing sand into the main line without clearing it just moves the problem. Ask the plumber where the sand ends up.

How to keep sand out of your pipes

Rinse sandy items outside with a hose. Use a mesh strainer in tubs and utility sinks. Empty buckets of rinse water onto the lawn, not down a drain.

If your basement has a floor drain, keep a cover on it and sweep debris away from it. Clean the sump pit once a year, and check the discharge line. If a drain keeps slowing, our drain cleaning team can clear it and look for the cause.
