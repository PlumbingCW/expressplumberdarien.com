A shower that drips after you shut it off is wasting water and staining the tub. Here is what is usually wrong inside the wall and how it gets fixed.

Why a shower faucet drips after you turn it off

A shower faucet drips because the part that stops the water no longer seals. In most homes that part is a cartridge or a rubber washer and seat inside the valve body, hidden behind the handle. Rubber hardens, plastic wears and mineral grit scratches the sealing faces.

The fix is usually to replace the worn part, not the whole faucet. A plumber shuts off the water, pulls the handle and trim, swaps the cartridge or the washer set and tests for a clean shutoff. If the valve body itself is pitted or the parts are no longer made, the valve is replaced instead.

Which case you have depends on the faucet type. The table below matches common styles to the usual cause.

Faucet style, likely cause and usual repair

Look at the handle. One lever that moves up and down and side to side is a different design from two separate knobs.

What you haveWhy it dripsUsual repair
Single lever or knobWorn or cracked cartridgeReplace the cartridge
Two handles, hot and coldRubber washer or seat on the dripping sideReplace washer set, reface or replace the seat

A drip on the hot side only often means a hot-side part is worn. A plumber confirms this once the trim is off.

How a plumber repairs a dripping shower valve

  1. 1

    Find the shutoff

    Water is turned off at a fixture stop if one exists or at the main shutoff for the house.

  2. 2

    Remove the trim

    The handle, escutcheon plate and stem cover come off, and the valve model is identified.

  3. 3

    Replace the worn part

    The cartridge or washer set is swapped. Seats are checked for pitting and cleaned or replaced.

  4. 4

    Test the shutoff

    Water comes back on slowly. Hot and cold are tested, then the drip is watched for a few minutes.

What you can safely check before calling

You can confirm where the drip comes from. Dry the tub, wait ten minutes and see whether water forms at the showerhead, the tub spout or around the handle. A drip at the handle plate points to a different failure than a drip at the showerhead.

Also look at the ceiling or wall on the other side of the bathroom. A stain, a soft drywall patch or a musty smell means water may be escaping inside the wall, which is more than a worn washer. In that case skip the repair attempt and read our leak detection page for how a hidden leak gets traced.

Do not force a handle that will not turn. Brass stems and old screws snap, and a broken stem turns a short repair into opening the wall.

Signs a drip is more than a worn part

Most drips are a simple repair. These signs mean the problem sits deeper.

  • ✓
    Water at the handle plateWater seeping around the trim can mean a failed valve body or a loose connection behind the tile.
  • ✓
    Low pressure along with the dripMineral buildup or a partly failed cartridge may be restricting flow.
  • ✓
    Stained ceiling below the bathroomWater has been escaping long enough to soak framing or drywall.
  • ✓
    Hot water only shows up as a dripA hot-side seat or stop may be failing, and the heater is working harder than it should.

If any of these match, call and tell dispatch. Our faucet and sink repair service covers shower and tub valves too.

What older Darien homes mean for shower valves

Darien was incorporated in 1969 by residents of the Marion Hills, Brookhaven, Farmingdale and Hinsbrook subdivisions, so many houses date from the 1960s and 1970s. Bathrooms from that period often still have their original valves, and some of those designs are no longer stocked as complete cartridges.

That affects your decision in two ways. First, ask the plumber whether a matching cartridge exists before agreeing to a quick repair. Second, if the valve is old and the wall is open for another reason, replacing the valve at the same visit can cost less in labor than coming back later.

Mineral buildup is a common reason cartridges wear faster. Always match a replacement cartridge to the valve maker's manufacturer guidelines, since cartridges are model specific.

Why fixing a small drip soon matters

A drip feels harmless, but it adds up. Constant moisture stains tubs, loosens caulk and can wet the framing behind the wall over months. A short repair now usually costs less than repairing a wall later.

Drips also hide bigger faults. A shower that drips because a stop is failing may be one rough use away from not shutting off at all. If you ever cannot stop the water, close the main shutoff and call right away. Our burst pipe repair page explains what to do while you wait.
