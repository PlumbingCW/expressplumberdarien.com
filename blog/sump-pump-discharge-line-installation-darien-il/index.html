Sump Pump Discharge Line Installation in Darien, IL

The pump gets the attention, but the discharge line decides whether the water actually leaves your property. Here is how a good line is planned and installed.

What a sump discharge line has to do

A sump pump discharge line carries water from the pit to a place where it will not come back. It leaves the pump, passes through a check valve, exits the house through the rim joist or wall and ends on the surface of your yard or at an approved outlet. A well-planned line moves water far enough from the foundation that it does not soak back into the basement.

In Darien, the City has told residents to keep sump pump discharge off the sanitary sewer and at least five feet from a neighbor's property. That is City guidance reported in 2020, so confirm the current rule by calling (630) 353-8105 before you plan the run.

The sections below cover where the line should end, what it is made of and how to keep it from freezing.

How a discharge line is installed

  1. 1

    Plan the route

    The plumber picks the shortest path to an outlet that slopes away from the foundation and avoids walkways.

  2. 2

    Set the pump connection

    Pipe is joined above the pump with a union, and the check valve goes in the vertical run.

  3. 3

    Make the wall exit

    A hole is drilled through the rim joist or wall and sealed around the pipe.

  4. 4

    Run and slope the line

    Outside pipe is laid with a steady downward slope so it drains between cycles.

  5. 5

    Test with water

    The pit is filled, the pump is run and the whole line is checked for leaks and for clean flow at the outlet.

Pipe, check valve and fittings

Most discharge lines use rigid plastic pipe with glued fittings and a union near the pump so the pump can be pulled for service. A check valve in the vertical run stops water from falling back into the pit when the pump shuts off. Without one, the pump cycles more often and wears faster.

Use pipe sized to match the pump outlet, not smaller. A narrower line raises back pressure and slows the pump. Flexible corrugated hose is sometimes used outdoors, but it holds water in its ridges and traps ice, so many plumbers prefer smooth rigid pipe.

If your pump is old or noisy, a new line is a good time to check it. Our sump pump installation and repair page covers pump replacement.

Discharge line mistakes we see

Most failures come from a few repeated decisions.

  • ✓
    The line ends too close to the houseWater soaks back down along the foundation and returns to the pit, so the pump runs in a loop.
  • ✓
    No slope on the outside runWater sits in the pipe, freezes in winter and blocks the next cycle.
  • ✓
    Discharge into a floor drain or laundry tubThat sends clean groundwater into the sewer system, which the City asks residents not to do.
  • ✓
    No check valveWater drains back after every cycle and the pump short-cycles.

Fixing one of these is usually a short job. Fixing basement flooding because the line froze is not.

Darien lots, storms and your discharge line

Darien grew from subdivisions such as Marion Hills, Brookhaven, Farmingdale and Hinsbrook in the 1960s, and many of those homes sit on basements with sump pits. Waterfall Glen Forest Preserve borders the south edge of the city, and low ground near it can hold water after a storm.

That matters for the discharge line in a practical way. A heavy rain can fill every pit on your street at once. If your neighbors' lines and yours all end near the same low spot, the water has nowhere to go. Direct your outlet so it flows away from both your foundation and the next property.

Sump pump work in Darien needs a building permit with a plat of survey. Ask Community Development at 1702 Plainfield Rd before you hire anyone, and make sure your plumber holds an Illinois license. Plumbing work in Illinois follows the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code), and pump makers publish manufacturer guidelines for check valves and pipe sizing.

Keeping the line working through winter

Ice is the main enemy of an outdoor discharge line in the Chicago suburbs. If the outlet freezes, the pump pushes against ice and may overheat. Use rigid pipe with a steady slope, keep the outlet clear of snow and consider a freeze-resistant emitter that opens when the line is blocked.

Check the outlet before every hard freeze and after any big thaw. If you see ice at the end of the pipe, clear it carefully. Do not chip at plastic with a metal tool.

Ongoing seepage or a wet basement wall may point to a bigger drainage problem. Our basement waterproofing page explains the plumbing side of that.
