Why a faucet drips, and what each type needs

A drip is a worn part letting water pass when the faucet is closed. The part depends on the faucet design.

Older compression faucets use rubber washers that harden. Cartridge faucets use a plastic or brass cartridge with seals. Ball and ceramic disc faucets use springs and discs that wear or collect mineral buildup. Identifying the type tells us which part to bring.

Stuck and leaking shutoff valves under the sink

The small valves under a sink are meant to be turned once a year or less, and many seize from mineral buildup. A valve that will not close, or weeps at the handle, is a repair on its own.

We replace the valve and its supply line together so the connection matches. If the valves are old, replacing both avoids a second call. Turning an old valve hard can crack it, so ask dispatch before you try.

Supply lines, braided hoses and slow leaks

Flexible supply lines connect the shutoff to the faucet. Over time, the hose or its nuts can fail, and the leak is often small and hidden at the back of the cabinet. A stain on the cabinet floor or a musty smell is a good sign.

We replace the line and tighten the connection to the proper snugness. If water has been getting into the cabinet for a while, check the wood for swelling. For a leak you cannot trace, our leak detection page explains the process.

Low pressure, sputtering and aerators

Weak flow at one faucet, but normal elsewhere, often means a clogged aerator or a worn cartridge. Unscrewing the aerator and rinsing it can restore the stream. Mineral buildup is a common cause, so ask your water utility for its water quality report or test your tap if you want to know more.

If the whole house has low pressure, the cause is elsewhere. A line problem might need water service line repair, and heavy mineral scale may suggest a look at water softener and filtration options.

Sink basins, drains and what is attached to them

A sink repair can mean more than the faucet. Drain strainers, pop-up stoppers, P-traps and slip nuts can leak or loosen. In the kitchen, the disposal and dishwasher hose also connect under the sink, so one leak can look like another.

If the disposal is the source, see garbage disposal repair. If the basin has a slow drain, our plumbers can cable the line the same visit. Tell dispatch what you see so the right parts come along. Cartridges and supply lines are fitted and tightened following manufacturer guidelines for that faucet.
