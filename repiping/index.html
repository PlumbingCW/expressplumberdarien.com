Repiping means removing worn water supply piping and putting new pipe in its place, usually through the walls, floors and basement ceiling. If a pipe is leaking now, call our 24/7 line at (630) 822-9290. For a planned repipe, dispatch will schedule a walk-through so you can see the plan.

While you decide, learn where your main shutoff is and mark it. Take photos of any stained ceilings or walls and note when the stains appeared. That timeline helps a plumber tell a one-time failure from a pattern.

Why a repipe is a pattern decision, not a single leak decision

One pinhole leak in a section of pipe does not mean the whole house needs new pipe. A second or third leak in different places, within a short period, often does.

The reason is that pipe wears from the inside. Water chemistry, flow speed and the age of the metal act on the whole system at once, so a failure in one spot says something about every other spot.

Signs your pipes may be near the end

No single sign proves the point. Several together are worth a plumber's time.

  • ✓
    Repeated leaksLeaks at different spots within a few seasons often mean the pipe material is failing, not just one joint.
  • ✓
    Brown or rusty waterWater that runs brown when you open a tap after a long pause can mean corrosion inside older steel pipe.
  • ✓
    Falling pressureNarrowing inside old pipes can reduce flow at showers and upstairs sinks.
  • ✓
    Green or white crustMineral crust on fittings and pipe surfaces is a leak or corrosion clue worth checking.
  • ✓
    Damp spots with no sourceStaining on a ceiling below a bathroom, with no drain leak, points to supply lines.

Copper and PEX compared for a Darien house

Both are accepted choices for supply piping. Which one fits depends on the house and your plan.

Many homes use a mix, such as copper at the meter and PEX in the walls. Ask the plumber what fits your layout.

How a repipe is staged, from first look to final test

  1. 1

    Walk-through

    A plumber maps the main shutoff, the meter, each fixture and the access points.

  2. 2

    Plan the routes

    We choose runs through the basement ceiling, closets and walls to limit wall openings.

  3. 3

    Water off, new pipe in

    Water is shut off in stages while new lines are run and connected, floor by floor.

  4. 4

    Pressure test

    The new system is tested for leaks before any wall is closed.

  5. 5

    Wall repair

    Small access holes are left for patching. Finish painting is a separate trade.

Spot repair, partial repipe or whole house

If only one branch has failed, such as the lines to a single bathroom, a partial repipe may fix the problem. A whole house repipe makes more sense when the same pipe runs through the entire house and has already failed in several places.

Before choosing, we will often suggest a leak detection visit to find hidden weak spots, or a burst pipe repair if water is already loose. Those steps show whether you have a local problem or a house-wide one.

New pipe, fittings and supports are chosen to meet the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code), and we follow the pipe maker's manufacturer guidelines for joints and spacing.

What decides the cost of a repipe

There is no flat figure for a repipe. These are the real factors, and we explain each when we walk the house.

Size of the houseNumber of bathrooms, kitchens and floors sets how much pipe is needed.

Wall repairOpening and patching drywall is part of the scope to talk about.
Fixture conditionOld shutoff valves and fixtures are often replaced at the same time.
Homeowners insurance usually does not pay for replacing pipe that is simply old. It may help with water damage from a sudden leak. Read your policy and keep photos and receipts.
