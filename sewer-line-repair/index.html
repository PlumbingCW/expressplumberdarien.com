What to do right now if the sewer line has failed

A failed sewer line gives clear warnings. These are the ones that mean you should call the same hour.

  • ✓
    Sewage in the lowest drainDark water or waste rising in a basement floor drain, shower or tub means the line is blocked below that point.
  • ✓
    Backups that return after cleaningIf a cable or jetter clears the line and it fails again within weeks, the pipe itself is probably damaged.
  • ✓
    A sinkhole or soft patch in the lawnSoil washing into a broken pipe leaves a void. A dip over the sewer path should be looked at promptly.
  • ✓
    Sewer smell indoors or in the yardA steady odor near a drain or along the lateral can point to a crack or a failed joint.

Do not pour chemical cleaners into a line you think is broken. They do not fix a crack, and they put caustic liquid in front of the next person who opens the pipe.

How we find out what is wrong first

We never guess at a sewer repair. A camera goes down the line from a cleanout, and you see the same video the plumber sees. It shows the exact spot of a crack, a root mass, an offset joint or a low section that holds water.

If the line is full of roots or debris, we clear it first so the camera can travel. Our sewer camera inspection service is the standalone version of this step, useful when you want a second look before deciding on a repair.

Common sewer line faults and how they get fixed

The right repair depends on what the camera finds. This table shows the usual pairing.

What the camera showsLikely causeTypical repair route
Fine roots entering at jointsJoints loosened with ageClear roots, then line or replace the section
Hairline crack along the pipeGround movement or old materialSpot repair or a liner if the pipe is otherwise sound
Pipe ends shifted apartSettling or a past dig nearbyExcavate and replace the offset section
Sagging section holding waterSoil settled under the pipeReplace the low section with proper slope
Flaking or collapsed pipe wallOld cast iron or clay tile at end of lifeReplace the damaged run, by dig or pipe bursting

This is a general guide. Pipe material, depth and what sits above the line decide the final method.

Dig repair, liner or pipe bursting

Open excavation is the most direct fix. The plumber digs to the damaged section, replaces it and backfills. It works well for a short, shallow repair and lets us correct slope at the same time.

A cured-in-place liner is a resin sleeve pulled through the existing pipe. It suits cracks and joint leaks in a pipe that is still round and mostly intact. Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe along the old path while breaking the old one outward. Both reduce lawn and driveway damage, but neither fits every line. Whatever the method, pipe materials and slope are governed by the standards in the Illinois Plumbing Code (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code).

When cleaning is enough and when it is not

Not every backup means a repair. A one-time clog from paper or grease can be cleared with sewer line cleaning, and a camera afterward tells you if the pipe is healthy. If the same line returns to the clog within a short time, repair is usually the better spend than repeating the cleaning.
